BurnP3+ Cell2Fire Simulation
Monte Carlo fire spread simulation using the Cell2Fire engine with CFFDRS FBP System fire behaviour equations

How It Works

Cell2Fire is a cell-based fire spread simulator that propagates fire across a raster grid using the Canadian Forest Fire Behaviour Prediction (FBP) System equations. Each cell's rate of spread, head fire intensity, and crown fire behaviour are computed from its fuel type, topography, and the input fire weather conditions.

BurnP3+ wraps Cell2Fire in a Monte Carlo framework: hundreds of fires are ignited at random locations across the landscape under defined weather scenarios. The burn probability for each cell is the proportion of iterations in which that cell burned. This produces a spatially explicit probability surface showing where fire is most likely to spread — not just where it might start.

The simulation accounts for fuel continuity, topographic slope and aspect effects on spread rate, and the transition between surface and crown fire in conifer stands. Fires that ignite in non-fuel cells do not spread. Fires in grass fuels spread rapidly but at low intensity; fires reaching C-2 (spruce) or M-2 (mixedwood) fuels can crown and generate the highest head fire intensities.

Two Scenarios: Standard vs. High HFI

Two fire weather scenarios bracket the range of wildfire risk:

ScenarioConditions
Standard Mean seasonal FFMC, ISI, and BUI from Edmonton-area stations. Most ignitions self-extinguish or remain small. Baseline probability under normal summer conditions.
High HFI 90th+ percentile FFMC, ISI, and BUI reflecting drought and high-wind events. Fires spread rapidly through grass corridors and crown into forested ravines, producing the high-probability corridors visible along the river valley.

The contrast between scenarios shows how sensitive Edmonton's urban-wildland interface is to fire weather escalation — the same fuel landscape produces dramatically different risk profiles.

Classification Method

Individual tree crowns were segmented from the 2025 leaf-on LiDAR point cloud and classified as conifer or deciduous using a Random Forest model (v2, 90.4% accuracy).

FBP fuel types were assigned within UPLVI Vegetated and Naturalized Area polygons (Edmonton Open Data) at 20m × 20m cell resolution in EPSG:3776.

  • ≥75% conifer crowns per cell → C-2 (Boreal Spruce)
  • ≥75% deciduous crowns → D-2 (Green Aspen)
  • 25–75% conifer → M-2 (Boreal Mixedwood Green) + percent conifer grid
  • <2 trees per cell → O-1b (Standing Grass)
  • Outside natural areas → NF (Non-fuel)

Spruce is Edmonton's dominant conifer, so C-2 was selected over C-3. Leaf-on green variants (D-2, M-2) are used; substitute D-1/M-1 for leaf-off spring scenarios.

Technical Notes

Input Data

  • LiDAR: 2025 City of Edmonton Vegetation LiDAR (leaf-on acquisition)
  • Natural Areas: UPLVI Vegetated + Naturalized Areas (Edmonton Open Data)
  • Tree Classifier: Random Forest v2 conifer/deciduous (90.4% accuracy)
  • Elevation: LiDAR-derived DEM for slope/aspect inputs to FBP spread equations
  • Fire Weather: Edmonton-area station data (FFMC, ISI, BUI, wind speed/direction)
  • CRS: EPSG:3776 (NAD83 / Alberta 3TM ref merid 114 W)
  • Cell Size: 20m × 20m

Assumptions & Limitations

  • Fuel classification is based on natural area boundaries, not spectral grass detection — grass outside UPLVI/Naturalized polygons is classified NF
  • Urban canopy trees outside designated natural areas are classified as NF (non-fuel), excluding private property and boulevard trees from the fuel grid
  • Percent conifer raster is provided for M-2 cells as required BurnP3 PC input
  • Suppression response is not modelled — all fires burn freely until extinguished by fuel discontinuity or duration limits
  • Wind direction is held constant within each iteration; gustiness and wind shifts are not simulated
  • Spotting (ember transport) is not included in the current Cell2Fire configuration
